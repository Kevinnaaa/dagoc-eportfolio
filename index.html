About

I build things
that stay out
of the way.

I'm Brendan Jadrian Dagoc — a designer and front-end developer. I started out making posters and zines, then fell into web design because it let me ship the same idea without waiting on a printer. Today I work mostly with small studios and independent brands who want something considered, fast, and easy to maintain.

Good interfaces are quiet. If you notice them, something is probably wrong. — Brendan Dagoc

That means: fewer dependencies, semantic HTML first, design tokens instead of one-off values, and a real conversation about what the site is *for* before opening Figma. I've shipped work for editorial teams, product companies, and one very patient ceramics studio. I write about process occasionally and answer emails faster than I should.

What I do

Five disciplines.
One designer.

I work across design and code because the handoff is where most good ideas die. The disciplines overlap — a colour decision is a code decision is a content decision. Keeping all three in one head is how the work stays coherent.

01 / Interface design

Websites and web apps
that feel considered.

Layout, typography, motion, and interaction — designed in Figma, built in code, tested on real devices. I design for the content you actually have, not the placeholder you wish you had.

For studios · startups · independent brands
02 / Front-end

Semantic, fast, maintainable.

HTML, CSS, vanilla JS, and modern frameworks when they earn their weight.

03 / Design systems

Tokens, components, docs.

A single source of truth so the next person doesn't have to guess.

04 / Art direction

Visual identity for the web.

Type pairing, colour systems, imagery direction — the parts that make a site feel like *this* brand and not the template it started from.

05 / Prototyping

Clickable, honest prototypes.

Enough fidelity to make a real decision — no more, no less.

06 / Collaboration

Working with your team.

I plug into existing workflows, review PRs, and document as I go.

07 / Care & maintenance

Handover, docs,
and long-term care.

Every project ships with written documentation, a README that a new developer can actually follow, and a short loom walkthrough of the tricky parts. I stay available for questions after launch — most of my work comes from people I've already built something for.

How I work

From first call
to first deploy.

Every project starts with a short call — no pitch deck, no template proposal. I ask what the site is supposed to do, who's going to maintain it, and what "done" looks like. Most projects run four to eight weeks from kickoff to launch; the bottleneck is almost never the build, it's waiting on content and decisions. I keep that loop tight by writing things down early.

  • Step 01Discovery — goals, audience, constraints, references
  • Step 02Structure — sitemap, content model, wireframes
  • Step 03Design — type, colour, layout, components
  • Step 04Build — semantic HTML, CSS, JS, CMS if needed
  • Step 05Test — devices, accessibility, performance
  • Step 06Launch — deploy, docs, handover walkthrough
